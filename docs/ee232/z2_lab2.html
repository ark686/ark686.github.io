1. Introduction ​

This lab will investigate the equivalence of NOR/AND and NAND/NOR, product-of-sums and sums-of-products circuits, full-adder, ripple-carry adder, and adder/subtractor.

5.6 Ripple Adder ​

An n-bit “ripple” adder can be constructed using n full-adders to add two n-bit input numbers xn−1xn−2⋯x0 and yn−1yn−2⋯y0 as shown in Figure 7. It is referred to as a ripple adder as carries may need to ripple (propagate) from lower bits to upper bits (depending on the numbers being added). This limits the maximum speed of this type of adder for larger values of n.

  1. Construct a 4-bit ripple adder in Circuit JS:

5.7 Adder/Subtractor ​

A standard adder works correctly for adding positive and negative numbers if those numbers are encoded as two’s complement. A number can also be subtracted from another number by converting the second number to a negative number (i.e. effectively multiple by −1) and then adding it.

A circuit which performs such actions is shown in Figure 9. The “−add/subtract” signal controls if performing X+Y (when −add/subtract=0) or X−Y (when −add/subtract=1). It does this by converting Y to a two’s complement negative value (flipping all bits of Y and adding 1).

One issue of concern is determining if the operation has “overflowed” (the resulting sum is incorrect as there are not enough bits in S to hold the answer). One method to determine overflow is the following algorithm:

If the sign of two numbers into an adder are the same but the sign of the sum is different, then an overflow has occurred.
